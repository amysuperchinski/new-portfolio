## Overview

IcPay is a fintech offering digital business accounts, cards and payments. I worked with the company across two periods between 2020 and 2025: first as its **sole designer**, creating the brand and website that positioned a young company in front of customers and investors; then, after returning as **Senior Product Designer**, designing the product itself — the desktop and mobile banking experience and the design system underneath it.

## Phase 1 — A Brand That Builds Trust

### The Challenge

A young fintech has to convince two audiences at once: customers deciding whether to trust it with their money, and investors deciding whether to back it. IcPay’s early brand and website didn’t do either convincingly.

### What I Did

- **Led the rebrand end to end**, defining the new visual identity and the guidelines that kept it consistent across every surface — from the website to cards and marketing.
- **Rebuilt the website’s information architecture**, then took it through wireframes and prototypes to final UI, owning design direction across concurrent projects in a small, fast-moving team.
- **Established the first shared UI foundations** — type scale, color tokens and reusable components — which later became the basis of the product’s design system.

## Phase 2 — Designing the Product

When I returned to IcPay, the goal had changed: take the company from a brand and a website to a working financial product, on desktop and mobile.

### Core Financial Flows

Banking flows carry real constraints — security, compliance and fraud prevention — while many users were opening a business account for the first time. I designed the core flows (payments, cards, bills and account management) to keep those safeguards in place without making the product feel complicated.

### A Design System as a Product

I built and maintained IcPay’s design system — components, tokens and usage guidelines — and treated it as a product with its own users: the designers and developers building on it. It grew from the foundations created during the rebrand, which kept the brand and the product visually connected.

### Decisions Driven by Data

I tracked UX metrics on priority flows and used them to decide what to improve next, so iterations were sequenced by evidence rather than opinion.

### Accessible by Default

Accessibility was part of the definition of done across desktop and app: color contrast, visible focus states, clear hierarchy and comfortable touch targets.

## The Business Account Overview

The overview is the screen business owners open most: it has to answer *“How is my money doing, and is there anything I need to deal with?”* in a few seconds.

Key decisions in this concept:

- **Money first, account details second.** Balance and cash flow lead the page; account numbers move to the profile menu, with a quick “share account details” action next to “Add money”.
- **Surface what needs action.** A bill that needs attention is flagged at the top instead of waiting at the bottom of a list.
- **One clear time period.** A single period selector drives every metric, and changes are written in plain language (“3,8% less than last month”) as well as arrows and color.
- **Less noise.** Secondary currency conversions only where they help, category icons for faster scanning, and aligned numbers in every list.
- **Privacy and accessibility built in.** “Hide values” masks every amount on the page, and all text meets WCAG AA contrast.
